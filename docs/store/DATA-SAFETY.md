## 5. Flags: where the audit and the form disagree or force a judgment

- **A. ESPN cookies "shared" with ESPN?** The audit calls ESPN "a data recipient acting as the user's own service". Google's definition of sharing is any transfer to a third party, with exemptions for service providers (ESPN is not one), legal requests, anonymised data, and two alternative routes for transfers the user drives: "based on a specific user-initiated action, where the user reasonably expects the data to be shared" **or** "based on a prominent in-app disclosure and consent". Sending ESPN's own session cookie back to ESPN to read and write the lineup the user asked us to protect fits the user-initiated route, so **Shared = No**; that answer does not depend on the disclosure screen. Separately, the User Data policy requires a prominent disclosure and tap-to-agree consent before collecting authentication data for background use (research doc lines 230-246), and the audit found no such screen before cookie capture (`app/connect/espn.tsx:199-200` and `provider.tsx:29` are passive text). Add the disclosure-plus-Agree screen for that reason. If the owner prefers the conservative reading, declare Shared = Yes to ESPN with purpose App functionality, which is also accepted.
- **B. No form category for third-party login tokens.** The policy research proposed "User IDs / Other"; the audit lists the cookies as a credential. Google's "Other info" is defined as "Any other personal information such as date of birth, gender identity, veteran status, etc.", which is a worse fit than User IDs (account ID). Use **Personal info > User IDs** and let the privacy policy do the naming. Do not file the cookies under Device or other IDs.
- **C. Per-type encryption.** The audit and the policy research answer encryption per data type; the live form asks it once (confirmed on Google's page). The single Yes is correct because every transfer is HTTPS.
- **D. "Deletable" vs reality.** Settings > "Delete my account" now deletes the email and the auth user along with everything else (2026-10-10). The deletion answer is Yes once it has been exercised against the hosted project at least once to confirm the cascade behaves as the migration says; submitting Yes before that check, or before the public URL is hosted, is still an inaccurate form.
- **E. Account-deletion scope.** Google does not address anonymous/guest sessions; its only carve-out is accounts "created and operated offline", which a Supabase anonymous user is not. Both research agents and this answer treat the app as in scope because the email claim creates a server-side identity. Do not rely on the anonymous session as an exemption.
- **F. Push token sharing.** The audit asked whether Expo/FCM transfers count as sharing. Google's page: an "entity that processes user data on behalf of the developer and based on the developer's instructions" is a service provider, not sharing. Shared = No. The token is still "collected" and must be declared even though Android push does nothing until `google-services.json` is added (declaring a type that is not yet live is allowed; omitting one that is live is not). Category placement under Device or other IDs is an inference [VERIFY].
- **G. Roster data category.** The EAS research said "in-app actions", the policy research said "Other user-generated content". Neither Google definition is exact for a roster pulled from ESPN. The table splits it: team/league/roster under Other user-generated content, settings and decisions under Other actions. Declaring both is safe; declaring neither is not, because the rows exist on the server.
- **H. Required vs optional.** The research marked the user id "required" and email "optional"; the audit confirms both (anonymous users get Alerts and Ask Me). The ESPN session and roster are also Required: there is no use of the app without a connected league.
- **I. Things the audit found that the form has no slot for**: no retention periods implemented; orphaned anonymous `auth.users` (left behind by `signin.tsx`) and stale `devices` rows are never cleaned up; `actions.result`/`activity.data` may hold ESPN response text (`client.ts:244-249` keeps status/type/message; `_lib/store.ts:284` stores it). These belong in the privacy policy's retention section, not the form.
- **J. Internal testing.** Google's page confirms the form is not required while the app is only on the internal track, so the Saturday sideload/internal build is unaffected. Closed testing (the 12-tester, 14-day run) cannot start without the form, the privacy policy URL, and the account-deletion URL.
- **K. WebView collection.** Google counts a WebView as collection when the app controls its code or behaviour; the sign-in screen injects a polling script. Only the two cookies are read by it, and they are declared; the ESPN password is typed into ESPN's page and never read by app code.
